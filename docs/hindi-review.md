# Hindi content review sheet

This sheet lists **every Hindi string** shown by the Deoria Guide, next to its English source, so a native Hindi speaker can review it in one pass. It covers 354 strings: **62 in Priority 1** (emergency and governance) and **292 in Priority 2** (everything else).

The tables were generated mechanically from the JSON files, so check the source files rather than this sheet if they disagree. The "Questions flagged by the preparer" section at the end was written by a **non-native preparer**. Treat it as a list of things to look at, not as corrections.

## How to review

**What to check in each Hindi string**

1. **Accuracy.** Does it say the same thing as the English? Look for meaning that was dropped, added or changed. Helplines and official roles matter most here.
2. **Natural, everyday Hindi.** Use words people in Deoria actually use and would understand at a glance, including in an emergency. Avoid heavily Sanskritised or machine-translated phrasing. A common English loanword such as एम्बुलेंस, कॉल or पेज is fine if that is what people say.
3. **Spelling.** Check matras, anusvara/chandrabindu (ं / ँ), halant, and nukta (ज़, फ़, ख़, ग़). Proper nouns (people, villages, temples, officials) should match how they are written locally or on official sources.
4. **Consistent terms.** The same English term should be translated the same way everywhere, unless there is a reason not to.
5. **Numbers unchanged.** Phone numbers, helpline numbers, years, dates, PIN/STD codes and distances must be identical to the English. (An automated check found no digit mismatches between `en` and `hi` at the time of writing, but please confirm by eye in Priority 1.)
6. **Punctuation.** Use `।` (purna viram) to end Hindi sentences. Keep `—`, `·` and `…` where they are, because the layout relies on them.

**How to mark changes**

- **Option A (preferred): edit this file.** In the last column, write `OK`, or write the full corrected Hindi text. Add a short reason in brackets if it is not obvious. Then send the file back or open a pull request.
- **Option B: use the site.** Open the "Suggest a correction" form (About page) or a GitHub issue, and quote the **#** and the **key path** from this sheet, for example `#5 emergency.json[4].label`.
- Do not change the English column. If the English itself looks wrong, add a note.
- Key paths: `i18n x.y` means key `x.y` in `src/i18n/hi.json` (English in `src/i18n/en.json`). `file.json[3].name` means item 3 (counting from 0) of `src/data/file.json`, field `name`, inside its `hi` value. The small grey text after a path is the item's slug, id or phone number, to help you find it.

**Priority order**

1. **Priority 1a: Emergency.** People may read these under stress, and a wrong or unclear word here can cost time. Review these first and most carefully.
2. **Priority 1b: Governance.** Official titles, names of representatives and the status badges. These must be correct and respectful.
3. **Priority 2.** All other UI text and content, grouped by file. Review in any order. Short labels (navigation, buttons) appear on every page, so they are worth a careful look.
4. **Preparer's questions** (end of document). Go through these after, or alongside, the tables.

The summary (`places.json[6].summary`) and `photos.json[19].caption` use `गोपुरम`. Also, `आरती और दर्शन का समय मंदिर की सूचना से पूछ लें` ("ask the notice") reads oddly. 26. **`audiences.json[2].items[3].body`: `निकटतम विश्वविद्यालय शहर`.** This is a literal calque of "nearest university city".
27. **`i18n search.resultsFor`: "Results for" → `परिणाम`.** In Hindi the query would normally come first (`"X" के लिए परिणाम`). Please check how it reads on the search page, where the query is shown after this label.

### D. Consistency of terms and spelling

31. **Road: `रोड` vs `मार्ग`.** Kasia Road is `कसया रोड` in `places.json[5]` but `देवरिया–कसया मार्ग` in `photos.json[5].caption`. Deoria–Barhaj Road is always `मार्ग` (`places.json[4]`, `photos.json[9]`). Please choose one convention, ideally whatever is written on local signboards.
32. **Photo: `तस्वीर` vs `फ़ोटो`.** `तस्वीर/तस्वीरें` appears in `i18n sections.galleryNote`, `i18n photos.creditsIntro` and `photos.json[2]`, `[15]`, `[16]`. `फ़ोटो` appears in `i18n photos.photoBy`, `photos.credits`, `photos.noPhoto` and `places.json[6].tips`. Mixing them is probably fine, but please confirm.
33. **City centre:** `शहर का केंद्र` (`places.json[10].area`), `शहर के केंद्र में` (`places.json[10].summary`), `शहर के बीच` (`transport.json[0].body`) and `शहर के बीच में` (`audiences.json[0].items[0].body`).
34. **Nukta consistency.** `ज़` and `फ़` always have the nukta (`ज़िला` ×23, `फ़ोन`, `फ़ॉर्म`, `हज़ारों`, `आज़माएँ`, `रोडवेज़`). `ग़` and `ख़` never do: `गलत` (`i18n chat.disclaimer`, `i18n about.correctionIntro`), `तारीख` (`i18n about.intro`), `गजेटियर` (`city.json.nameOrigin`). Both are common in Hindi. Please decide whether to keep this mix or apply one rule.
35. **Anusvara vs half-nasal.** The text uses `एम्बुलेंस` (half म) but `संपूर्ण`, `संबद्ध` and `मंदिर` (anusvara), and `हिन्दी` rather than `हिंदी` (`i18n chat.greeting`, `city.json.languageNote`, plus the language switcher in `src/components/header.tsx`). The Central Hindi Directorate standard prefers anusvara (`एंबुलेंस`, `हिंदी`), but `एम्बुलेंस` and `हिन्दी` are very common. Please choose one.
36. **Abbreviations.** Abbreviations are written in three styles: with dots (`मोती बी.ए.`, `पं.`), as transliterated Devanagari without dots (`पीजी`, `आईएएस`, `आरटीओ`, `एसटीडी`, `जीआईसी`), and in Latin (`GIC` in `places.json[17].summary` and `audiences.json[2].items[2].body`, even though `places.json[17].name` is `जीआईसी देवरिया`). Please check whether this should be unified.
37. **Latin script inside Hindi strings.** Station and airport codes (`DEOS`, `BTT`, `GOP`, `KBK`), the vehicle code `UP-52` and `AI` (`i18n chat.disclaimer`) are probably right to keep, because they appear on tickets and number plates. **`places.json[12].area` = `UPSRTC` is identical to the English**: should it be `यूपी रोडवेज़` / `उ.प्र. राज्य सड़क परिवहन निगम`? `transport.json[2].head` already says `रोडवेज़ बस अड्डा`.
38. **`hi` identical to `en`.** Besides `UPSRTC` and `BTT` (`places.json[11].area`), the identical values are numbers or codes (`city.json.facts[1..4].value`, `city.json.timeline[3..6].period`). These are expected. The numbers use Western digits throughout. Please confirm this is preferred over Devanagari digits (०–९). The preparer assumes Western digits are right for phone numbers.
39. **Punctuation.** All Hindi sentences end with `।`, and no Hindi sentence ends with an ASCII `.` (checked automatically). The only ASCII dots are inside abbreviations (`बी.ए.`, `पं.`). No action expected.
